## 1. Business goal and problem statement
SwimQuest turns verified swimming activity into a shared team expedition: players join a
tournament, join a team (per Q-45: chosen from the organizer's teams), log verified swims and watch their team progress through a
stylized ocean world from a chosen start beach to the shared finish at Lý Sơn. Organizers run
multiple tournaments and seasons (SRC-3 §1). The pack expands the product from Route 01 gameplay
into a full platform: accounts, profiles, tournaments, teams, discovery/joining, start selection,
verified activity and progress, leaderboards/badges, organizer/admin controls, analytics, privacy
and safety (SRC-1 §Product scope; SRC-13 §Product decisions).

Success signals named in the sources: signup/verification completion, tournament
create-to-publish and join conversion, team participation, verification pass rates and latency,
weekly active swimmers, route finish rate by start baseline (SRC-10 §Metrics); a small pilot with
a fairness/safety review (SRC-4 §P6). No numeric targets or launch date are given (G-21).

## 6. Business rules and data entities
- BR-01: Lý Sơn is the only finish and the shared finish for all teams; it cannot be a start (SRC-7).
- BR-02: Each team selects exactly one start among the first six destinations; selection locks at the configured deadline/season start; afterwards only an audited exceptional correction (SRC-7). A team with no start at the deadline is assigned the start the fewest teams have, ties at random, and that start locks the same way (Q-57).
- BR-03: Progress = min(100, eligible units / route length units × 100), server-computed, fixed precision, display rounding only (SRC-7).
- BR-04: Only verified activities contribute; pending, rejected, duplicate, disputed and revoked do not (SRC-7 §Activity status).
- BR-05: A verified activity contributes once only (SRC-9 #25).
- BR-06: The route distance is published/reviewed before season start and cannot be changed silently after it starts (SRC-3 §7; SRC-8).
- BR-07: ~~Routes are not ranked by raw distance when baselines differ (SRC-3 §7).~~ Superseded by BR-17: baselines no longer differ (Q-06).
- BR-17: Every start requires the same swim distance to Lý Sơn; the start choice changes only the scenery and the path on the map (Q-06).
- BR-18: Swims from trusted sources (GPX/FIT upload in release 1; Strava, Garmin, Coros, Suunto once built) are accepted by automatic checks; swims that fail a standard check are flagged and go to the organizer's review queue (Q-04, Q-34, Q-36).
- BR-19: A manual swim entry never counts until a person approves it (Q-03, Q-04). Applies when manual entry is built in a later release (Q-38).
- BR-20: In release 1 an email/password account is active immediately, without email verification (Q-01).
- BR-21: An account may create at most 3 tournaments over its lifetime; more only after a platform admin approves the player's request (Q-08).
- BR-22: One verified swim counts for every tournament the player is active in on the swim's date, toward the player's team in each (Q-09).
- BR-23: Pool and open-water swims count the same (Q-37).
- BR-24: Flagged swims and disputes of a tournament (and manual entries, once built) are reviewed by that tournament's organizer or the review helpers the organizer appoints (Q-35, Q-38).
- BR-25: Organizer extra rules apply only to their own tournament and can only be stricter than the tournament's realistic swim limits (Q-36, Q-56). The realistic swim limits are set per tournament by the organizer and must stay inside the platform-wide sanity bounds set by a platform admin (Q-56).
- BR-38: A swim outside a tournament's realistic swim limits is flagged to that tournament's review queue; a swim that misses an extra rule does not count there and can only be disputed (Q-56 b, BR-27). The realistic limits lock at season start (Q-56 c).
- BR-26: Teams are created by the tournament's organizer, each with its own member limit; the organizer moves players between teams and appoints the captain; every such action is audited (Q-11).
- BR-27: A trusted-source swim that misses a tournament's extra rule does not count in that tournament and is not reviewed; the player is told which rule it missed and may dispute (Q-41).
- BR-28: When a swim needs a person's review, each tournament it counts for decides for itself; it may count in one and not in another (Q-42).
- BR-29: Scoring (Q-43, accepted proposal): 10 points per verified km (1 per full 100 m); +20 for the first verified swim in the tournament; +10 for a swim ≥ 1,000 m or +25 instead for ≥ 3,000 m, at most one long-swim bonus per player per day; weekly consistency multiplier on distance points only (2 active days ×1.10, 3+ ×1.20, +5% per earlier consecutive 3+ week, max ×1.40), added when the week ends; one daily limit per player (tournament setting, default 10 km) caps both the distance that counts and the distance points (Q-48); team points = sum of members' points; a tournament may change these numbers until its season starts; personal XP uses the same rules with the platform values, each swim once; revoking a swim removes its points, bonuses and XP and recalculates the week's multiplier.
- BR-30: Team points, not verified km, move the ship; a team finishes when its points reach the route length in points (BR-34). Every start has the same route length (BR-17 still holds).
- BR-34: Route length in points = route km × the tournament's points per km (default 10), so plain swimming moves the ship at real speed (100 m swum = 100 m of route) and bonuses and multipliers make it faster (Q-48).
- BR-35: Once the tournament has started, a player cannot leave their team or switch teams on their own (Q-50). The rule binds only players' own actions: the organizer's moves (BR-31) and captain/organizer removals (BR-37) remain allowed (Q-53).
- BR-37: A member removed by the captain or organizer after the start leaves the tournament; the swims they made stay with the team, with their points (Q-53). Before the start a removed player stays in the tournament without a team and may choose another team with room (A-19, confirmed by Q-53).
- BR-36: Choosing a team that has room joins it at once; nobody approves it (Q-49).
- BR-31: When the organizer moves a player, the player's earlier swims in that tournament, with their points, move to the new team (Q-12).
- BR-32: A finished team that drops below 100% loses its finish and Lý Sơn badge until it reaches 100% again (Q-13).
- BR-33: A tournament has one date range; joining is open from publishing until the season ends (Q-22). The range's start and end are date-times in Vietnam time; a swim is in the season when its start time is inside the range (Q-58).
- BR-08: Score domains are separate and never substituted silently (SRC-7).
- BR-09: Organizer role does not grant admin; captains cannot alter verified scoring records; organizers cannot silently change active baselines or rewrite verification (SRC-5).

## 11. Risks and external dependencies
- R-01: Strava API terms, scopes, rate limits, branding and data-display/storage rules may restrict sign-in or activity import; must be verified before building (SRC-5, SRC-2). Strava approval/app registration is an external dependency. Not a release 1 risk: Strava is "coming soon" only (Q-33, Q-34).
- R-11: Without email verification (Q-01), a player can register extra accounts to get past the 3-tournament limit (Q-08). Admin moderation (FR-54) is the only control in release 1.
- R-13: Team-change exploit: Q-12 moves a player's swims and points with them, so moving a strong swimmer can push a team over the finish (and pull another below it, Q-13). Players cannot switch on their own after the start (Q-50), but the organizer still can (Q-53); this is the stakeholder's choice. Every move is audited.
- R-14: Because points (with bonuses and multipliers) move the ship (Q-43 C, Q-48), teams that swim often reach Lý Sơn with noticeably fewer real km (about 70-90% faster than the km swum with the default values), and organizers who change point values (FR-78 item 7) change season length. Organizers need this explained when they set the route distance.
- R-12: Because one swim counts for several tournaments (Q-09), each tournament has its own extra rules (Q-36, Q-41) and its own reviewers (Q-42), the same swim can count in one tournament and not in another, and a flagged swim may wait in several review queues; players need a clear per-tournament status to avoid confusion.
- R-15: Without manual entry (Q-38), a player can only take part by uploading a GPX/FIT file: players who swim without a watch or swim app cannot record swims at all, and pool swims exported as GPX may carry no track or distance (FIT usually does). The pilot audience should be told before they join; the build must check what pool-swim exports from common watches contain.
- R-16: Invite links and announcements shared in a Zalo group open first in Zalo's built-in browser, where installing the PWA and turning on push may not work; RunnerWars' player guide explains how to leave it (SRC-16). Players may need the same help.
- R-17: PWA push on iPhone needs iOS 16.4 or later and the app added to the Home Screen (SRC-16); players who do not install the app only see in-app notifications.
- R-18: A Zalo group may include people who are not in the tournament; per-swim and per-player messages (Q-55 option A, chosen with this risk stated in the question) show players' names and swim data there, outside the privacy settings of FR-16. Mitigations: the organizer can switch those message kinds off; players are told (A-24).
- R-02: Scope is very large (9 modules, 23 screens, ~69 FRs, a full art pipeline) and Q-01 keeps all of it in release 1. Delivery to staging will take many stories; mitigation: order stories so a playable core reaches staging first (A-10).
- R-03: Art quality (Q-19: art is cut from the concept board). The board is a single 1536×1024 raster image: most items are roughly 40-100 px across and sit on a light card background with labels underneath, so cut-outs need background removal and will look soft when enlarged or on high-density screens; the seven landmarks are painted into the map rather than isolated (SRC-12 asks for 512-768 px isolated landmarks); the map has destination names painted in (conflicts with NFR-10); swimmers are a few small poses, not 8-frame loops (SRC-12); missing items: app icon/favicon, ≥8 team emblems (4 shown), default avatars, map marker states, ocean tile, energy orb, sparkle/milestone glow effects, tournament banner template. The squad can crop and clean, not paint new art of the same style. Handling decided by Q-47: simple vector stand-ins in release 1, and the stakeholder designs replacements from the asset replacement list (FR-77).
- R-04: Fairness: reduced by Q-06 (equal distance for all starts). Remaining: bigger teams add more points per day (team points are a sum, Q-43); Q-11 lets the organizer set each team's size, so balance depends on the organizer.
- R-05: Safety and liability around open-water swimming encouragement; copy review needed (NFR-07).
- R-06: Personal data (location, health-like activity data, possibly minors): legal/privacy review and retention policy needed before public launch (SRC-5; G-12; G-14). Q-18 and Q-28 are deferred to that point; staging test data must not include real minors' data in the meantime.
- R-07: Verification integrity: Q-04 treats GPX/FIT uploads as trusted, but these files can be edited by hand before upload; automatic checks (Q-36) and duplicate detection reduce but do not remove this risk. GPX/FIT is the only intake in release 1 (Q-38), so this is the main integrity risk.
- R-08: Each external activity source (Strava, Garmin, Coros, Suunto) needs its own developer registration, approval and terms review. Apple Health cannot be read from a web app (Q-02 web only); Apple Watch users would have to export a GPX/FIT file. Release 1 avoids these: the sources are "coming soon" only (Q-34).
- R-09: Without email verification (Q-01), anyone can register with someone else's email. Password reset is done by a platform admin (Q-39), so the admin must check who is asking before resetting; with under 100 pilot players this is manual work the admin accepts.
- R-10: Every screen, message and email in two languages (Q-07) roughly doubles copy work and needs Vietnamese and English text reviewed (safety copy in particular, NFR-07).
